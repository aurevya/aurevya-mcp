## Section: Key Features of a Luxembourg Fund Platform (body)
**Page:** 12
**Layout:** White page. Two-line gold serif title top-left, Aurevya logo top-right. Three columns of body text; sub-headings in bold gold, secondary sub-headings in bold navy; one bulleted list.

**Content (verbatim, in reading order across the three columns):**

**Luxembourg société à responsabilité limitée (S.à r.l.)**
> A Luxembourg société à responsabilité limitée (S.à r.l.) is commonly used as the general partner (GP) of an SCSp because it combines operational flexibility with limited liability protection. Whilst the GP is responsible for managing and representing the partnership and would otherwise bear unlimited liability, using a Luxembourg S.à r.l. as the GP effectively ring-fences that exposure within a corporate entity. It also provides a familiar and efficient governance structure for management decision-making, contractual execution, regulatory oversight and operational substance, making it a practical and widely accepted vehicle for Luxembourg fund and private capital structures.

**The Legal Pillar: The Special Limited Partnership (SCSp)**
> The Société en Commandite Spéciale (SCSp) is the most frequent choice for alternative managers due to its contractual flexibility.

> Lack of Legal Personality: Unlike most continental European entities, the SCSp does not have a legal personality separate from its partners. This aligns it closely with the English and Delaware Limited Partnerships familiar to global investors.

> Contractual Freedom: The Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA) is the governing document. Almost all commercial terms—distribution waterfalls, voting rights, and management fees—can be freely negotiated without rigid statutory constraints.

> Confidentiality: The names of the Limited Partners (investors) and their specific capital commitments are not required to be made public in the Luxembourg Trade and Companies Register.

**The Regulatory Layer: The AIFM Framework**
> Most Luxembourg funds qualify as Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs). Their regulation typically happens at the "Manager" level rather than the "Product" level, creating two primary paths:

**A. The Registered (Sub-threshold) AIFM**
> For funds with assets under management (AuM) below €100m (with leverage) or €500m (unleveraged, closed-ended), a "lighter" regulatory regime applies.

> Benefits: Lower compliance costs and faster time-to-market.

> Limitations: Does not benefit from the "EU Marketing Passport" (marketing is typically done via National Private Placement Regimes).

**B. The Authorized AIFM (Full AIFMD)**
> For larger funds or those requiring a marketing passport.

> Benefits: Full "Passporting" rights to market to professional investors across all EU member states.

> Requirements: Requires a Luxembourg-based Depositary (Bank) and independent Audit.

**Key Commercial & Operational Features**
- Tax Transparency
- No Minimum Capital
- Asset Neutrality
- Umbrella Structures

**Global Distribution & Reputation**
> Triple-A Rating: Luxembourg's sovereign "AAA" rating provides a stable backdrop for long-term investment vehicles.

> The "Luxembourg Brand": Institutional investors (Pension funds, Insurance companies) often mandate that their alternative allocations be made through Luxembourg vehicles to ensure standardized reporting and governance.

> Service Provider Ecosystem: Access to a concentrated pool of specialized law firms, "Big Four" auditors, and professional fund administrators.

**1. The "Single-Asset AIF" Qualification**
> **Observation:** You have correctly identified that a single-asset vehicle can qualify as an Alternative Investment Fund (AIF) under Luxembourg law provided it raises capital from a number of investors with a defined investment policy.

> **Our Approach:** We will draft the Limited Partnership Agreement (LPA) to ensure the "Business Day" definition and "Investment Policy" preclude the vehicle from being classified merely as a passive holding company (Soparfi), ensuring it falls under the AIF regime as requested. This allows for the regulatory treatment you desire.

**2. The "Registered AIFM" Status (De Minimis)**
> **Observation:** You intend to operate as a "Registered AIFM" (Sub-threshold). This is appropriate provided assets under management (AUM) remain under €500M (unleveraged with 5-year lock-up) or €100M (with leverage).

> **Critical Note:** Please note that a "Registered AIFM" does not benefit from the EU Marketing Passport. Marketing to LPs outside Luxembourg will rely on National Private Placement Regimes (NPPR). We will include an assessment of this in our regulatory analysis.

**3. The Luxembourg-Bulgaria Nexus (Substance)**
> **Observation:** The acquisition of the Bulgarian entity creates a parent-subsidiary relationship.

> **Risk Mitigation:** To satisfy Luxembourg substance rules (Circular 18/698), the decision-making (portfolio management and risk management) must demonstrably happen in Luxembourg. The Bulgarian entity must remain strictly advisory. If the Bulgarian entity effectively runs the fund, the structure risks being deemed a "letterbox" entity.

> **Our Solution:** We will draft a robust Investment Advisory Agreement that creates a clear legal firewall, ensuring final decisions rest with the Luxembourg GP while leveraging Bulgarian due diligence capabilities.

**5. Deal-by-Deal Participation & Tracking Mechanics**

**Observation:**
> The proposed structure contemplates LP participation on a deal-by-deal basis, with different investors potentially participating in different transactions. This requires careful drafting to ensure that commitments, costs, liabilities and distributions are properly allocated by transaction.

**Risk Mitigation:**
> Without clear tracking mechanics, there may be uncertainty around whether non-participating LPs bear costs or risks linked to deals in which they did not participate. This may create investor disputes, accounting complexity and governance concerns.

**Our Solution:**
> We will ensure that the LPA provides robust deal-by-deal mechanics, including separate commitment schedules, capital call provisions, cost allocation rules, return distribution mechanics and investor participation records for each investment.
