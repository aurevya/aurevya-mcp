```
The initial licensing submission to the FSC describing the CIS's governance, investment strategy, promoters, and draft legal documents.

A disclosure document providing potential investors with the fund's objectives, risks, and terms. Prepared in accordance with FSC's disclosure guidelines.
```

```
Bilateral treaties that prevent income from being taxed in both Mauritius and another jurisdiction.
```

## Section: Collective Investment Scheme — A Comprehensive Overview (Page 1 of 2)

**Layout:** Two-line title, first line gold (`COLLECTIVE INVESTMENT SCHEME -`), second line navy (`A COMPREHENSIVE OVERVIEW`). Four text columns, no imagery.
**Content:**
```
LEGAL STRUCTURE AND LICENSING

A Collective Investment Scheme (CIS) in Mauritius is typically structured as a company incorporated under the Companies Act 2001. To operate as a global fund, the company must:

• Be licensed as a Global Business Company (GBC) under the Financial Services Act 2007;
• Obtain authorisation as a CIS under the Securities Act 2005.

This structure is referred to as an Investment Company, where the company's principal business is to invest its funds primarily in securities, with the aim of spreading investment risk and delivering returns to its shareholders.

In a self-managed CIS, the company does not appoint an external CIS Manager. Instead, the Board of Directors assumes full responsibility for investment management, governance, and compliance.

This model is particularly suited to experienced promoters or institutional investors seeking greater control and cost efficiency.

FUND CLASSIFICATION

Mauritian law recognises two main categories of global funds:

• Open-Ended Funds – also known as Collective Investment Schemes (CIS), where investors can redeem their shares at Net Asset Value (NAV);
• Closed-Ended Funds – commonly referred to as Private Equity Funds, where shares are not redeemable at NAV.

This guide focuses on the Open-Ended Fund, structured as a self-managed CIS.

REGULATORY APPROVAL PROCESS

Before commencing operations, a CIS must be approved by the Financial Services Commission (FSC). The FSC evaluates the application based on:

• The track record and credentials of the promoters;
• The governance and structure of the fund;
• The fund's investment objectives and strategy;
• The target investor base and markets;
• The types of assets to be invested in;
• The experience and regulatory standing of the custodian, administrator, and other service providers.

CUSTODY OF ASSETS

All CIS must appoint an FSC-approved custodian to safeguard the fund's assets. In this case, AfrAsia Bank will act as the custodian. AfrAsia is a well-established financial institution in Mauritius and the broader African region.

The custodian is responsible for:

• Holding the fund's assets in accordance with the CIS Regulations and the fund's constitutive documents;
• Executing a formal Custody Agreement;
• Delegating custody functions to sub-custodians (if necessary), subject to FSC approval, while retaining overall responsibility.

LOCAL SUBSTANCE REQUIREMENTS

The FSC expects the central administration of the CIS to be based in Mauritius. This includes:

• A local fund administrator;
• A local cash custodian;
• A local auditor approved by the FSC;
• Board meetings held in Mauritius and qualified local directors to demonstrate effective management and control.
• To benefit from tax incentives, the CIS must also meet economic substance requirements, including:
• Conducting core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Mauritius;
• Employing an adequate number of qualified personnel;
• Incuring minimum annual expenditure proportionate to the level of activity.
```

```
TAXATION AND PARTIAL EXEMPTION REGIME

Mauritius offers a competitive tax regime for Global Business Companies (GBCs), including Collective Investment Schemes (CIS). The standard corporate income tax rate is 15%, but qualifying funds may benefit from the Partial Exemption Regime (PER), which significantly reduces the effective tax burden.

PARTIAL EXEMPTION RATES FOR CIS
```

| Income Type | Partial Exemp-tion | Effective Tax Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest income** earned by a CIS or Closed-End Fund | **95%** | **0.75%** |
| **Other qualifying income** (e.g. advisory, management, or administrative services) | **80%** | **3%** |

```
Succession Planning

A trust is an efficient vehicle to ensure a smooth succession and planning for future generations.

A settlor, who has achieved a successful family business, can ensure that his assets and business continue for several generations as per his wishes and with the help of trustees.

Estate Planning

In the event of death, a trust enables anonymous and discrete transfer of assets often without probate and also avoiding inheritance tax.

Forced Heirship

There are few countries where the legislation dictates the specificities of wealth distribution post the death of the owner. In such cases, a trust avoids forced heirship in countries where legislation dictates wealth distribution as the trust prevails over the legislation.

Mitigation of Taxation Liabilities Transferring assets to a trust can be an effective method of reducing taxes. A Mauritian trust can choose to be tax resident in Mauritius and hold a global business license.

Such a trust benefits from multiple advantages in Double Taxation Treaties which Mauritius has. Furthermore, a trust where the settlor and beneficiaries are not resident in Mauritius, is not subject to taxation for income derived outside of Mauritius.

High Degree of Confidentiality

There is no requirement to disclose the trust assets and any other details to third parties. Hence, the privacy of the trust remains enclosed to the parties of the trust only.

Charitable and Philanthropic Purpose

Many High Net Worth Individuals (HNWIs) have personal interest in philanthropy and charity. A trust gives structure to donation and expense strategy enforcing the wishes of the settlor.

TRUST DEED OR TRUST INSTRUMENT

A trust is created by an instrument in writing and contain the name of the Trustee , the intention of the settlor to create the trust, the object of the trust, the name of the beneficiaries or class of beneficiaries, the property or asset being held and the duration of the trust.
```

### Page 17 — KEY PARTIES TO A TRUST

```
SETTLOR

A settlor is the person or entity who or which settles the assets into the trusts and decides in prior how the assets should be managed and used. The settlor may also be a trustee, a beneficiary, a protector or an enforcer, but shall not be the sole beneficiary

of a trust of which he is a settlor. In case where the Settlor is a corporate entity, there is no requirement for the Beneficiaries of the trust to be the corporate entity's shareholders or connected persons.

TRUSTEE

The primary duty of the trustee is to hold and manage the assets of the trust for the beneficiaries through the powers vested in him by the settlor and as defined in the trust deed. A trustee is under an equitable obligation to hold properties of the settlor for the benefit of the beneficiaries.

The trustees will be required to do various things with the trust property, for e.g. trustees have the duty to invest the trust property safely so that it makes reasonable returns, pay over the capital to a person for his life and so on.

A trust may comprise of a maximum of four Trustees, including at least one qualified trustee (licensed by the Financial Services Commission).

The terms of the trust can also provide for the appointment of a Custodian Trustee and a Managing Trustee.

The Custodian Trustee has the sole function of holding the trust assets and the same are managed by the Managing Trustee.

PROTECTOR

The trust deed can stipulate the appointment of a protector of the trust, whose function is to advise the trustee. A protector can be, any person of full age and of sound mind, including the settlor, or any corporate, partnership or group of persons, whether incorporate or unincorporate.

ENFORCER

A purpose trust requires the appointment of an enforcer whose duty is to enforce the trust accordingly to its terms and purpose. No person is allowed to act as trustee and enforcer of the same trust at any given time.

BENEFICIARIES

A beneficiary is the individual who is entitled to the assets of the trust. A settlor cannot be the sole beneficiary of the trust. The trust instrument is allowed to provide for the addition of a person as beneficiary, or for the exclusion from benefit of a beneficiary or to impose an obligation upon a beneficiary as a condition of benefit.
```

### Regulatory regime and licensing (Page 14)
```
LEGAL STRUCTURE AND LICENSING

A Collective Investment Scheme (CIS) in Mauritius is typically structured as a company incorporated under the Companies Act 2001. To operate as a global fund, the company must:

• Be licensed as a Global Business Company (GBC) under the Financial Services Act 2007;
• Obtain authorisation as a CIS under the Securities Act 2005.

This structure is referred to as an Investment Company, where the company's principal business is to invest its funds primarily in securities, with the aim of spreading investment risk and delivering returns to its shareholders.

In a self-managed CIS, the company does not appoint an external CIS Manager. Instead, the Board of Directors assumes full responsibility for investment management, governance, and compliance.

This model is particularly suited to experienced promoters or institutional investors seeking greater control and cost efficiency.
```

### Fund classification (Page 14)
```
FUND CLASSIFICATION

Mauritian law recognises two main categories of global funds:

• Open-Ended Funds – also known as Collective Investment Schemes (CIS), where investors can redeem their shares at Net Asset Value (NAV);
• Closed-Ended Funds – commonly referred to as Private Equity Funds, where shares are not redeemable at NAV.

This guide focuses on the Open-Ended Fund, structured as a self-managed CIS.
```

### Regulatory approval process (Page 14)
```
REGULATORY APPROVAL PROCESS

Before commencing operations, a CIS must be approved by the Financial Services Commission (FSC). The FSC evaluates the application based on:

• The track record and credentials of the promoters;
• The governance and structure of the fund;
• The fund's investment objectives and strategy;
• The target investor base and markets;
• The types of assets to be invested in;
• The experience and regulatory standing of the custodian, administrator, and other service providers.
```

```
CUSTODY OF ASSETS

All CIS must appoint an FSC-approved custodian to safeguard the fund's assets. In this case, AfrAsia Bank will act as the custodian. AfrAsia is a well-established financial institution in Mauritius and the broader African region.

The custodian is responsible for:
```

### Local substance requirements (Page 14)
```
LOCAL SUBSTANCE REQUIREMENTS

The FSC expects the central administration of the CIS to be based in Mauritius. This includes:

• A local fund administrator;
• A local cash custodian;
• A local auditor approved by the FSC;
• Board meetings held in Mauritius and qualified local directors to demonstrate effective management and control.
• To benefit from tax incentives, the CIS must also meet economic substance requirements, including:
• Conducting core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Mauritius;
• Employing an adequate number of qualified personnel;
• Incuring minimum annual expenditure proportionate to the level of activity.
```

```
TAXATION AND PARTIAL EXEMPTION REGIME

Mauritius offers a competitive tax regime for Global Business Companies (GBCs), including Collective Investment Schemes (CIS). PARTIAL EXEMPTION RATES FOR CIS
```

```
Conditions to Qualify for Partial Exemption
To benefit from the 80% or 95% exemption, the CIS must satisfy the economic substance requirements, which include:
```
