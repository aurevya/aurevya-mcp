# YOUR FAMILY OFFICE, YOUR LEGACY.
## 1. Understanding Family Offices

**WHAT IS A FAMILY OFFICE?**
A family office is a private organization established to manage the wealth, investments, and affairs of a high-net-worth family. It serves as a centralized platform for financial planning, governance, succession, philanthropy, and lifestyle management. Family offices are designed to preserve and grow wealth across generations while aligning with the family's values and long-term vision.

**TYPES OF FAMILY OFFICES IN MAURITIUS**
Mauritius offers two primary regulatory structures for family offices under the Financial Services Act 2007: the **Single Family Office (SFO)** and the **Family Office (Multiple) Licence (MFO)**. These structures are designed to support both private family wealth management and commercial multi-family service platforms, with clear regulatory expectations and operational requirements.

# YOUR FAMILY OFFICE, YOUR LEGACY.
## 1. Understanding Family Offices

**SINGLE FAMILY OFFICE (SFO)**

A Single Family Office is a private entity established to manage the wealth and affairs of one family. It is not licensed to provide services to third parties and is typically exempt from full regulatory licensing, provided it does not solicit or manage external funds.

**Key Features:**
- Serves only one family group
- Fully owned and controlled by the family
- Not open to the public or third-party clients
- May be exempt from licensing under certain conditions
- Must maintain internal governance, risk, and compliance protocols

**FAMILY OFFICE (MULTIPLE) LICENCE (MFO)**

A licensed MFO is a regulated entity that provides family office services to multiple unrelated families. It is governed under the Financial Services Act 2007 and must meet detailed licensing and operational criteria issued by the FSC.

**APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS**

**Applicants must submit:**
- A formal application under Section 14 of the Financial Services Act
- A comprehensive business plan including:
- Background of promoters and designated officers
- Organisation chart
- Investment strategy and service offerings
- Wealth management and preservation methodology
- Source of wealth documentation
- Three-year financial forecasts (P&L, cash flow, balance sheet)
- Statutory filings for incorporation in Mauritius
- Consent forms and personal documentation for all officers and controllers

## 5. Common Challenges and Mistakes in Setting Up a Family Office

**1. MOVING TOO FAST WITHOUT CLEAR PRIORITIES**
**The Issue:** Families may attempt to implement too many services or structures at once without a clear focus on core objectives.
**Impact:** This can lead to rushed decisions, fragmented operations, and a lack of strategic coherence.
**Solution:** Start with a clear mission and phased implementation plan. Prioritize foundational elements before expanding services.

**2. LACK OF STRUCTURAL FLEXIBILITY**
**The Issue:** Designing a rigid structure that doesn't account for future growth, generational change, or evolving regulations.
**Impact:** Inflexibility can hinder adaptation to new needs, technologies, or market conditions.
**Solution:** Build with both short- and long-term goals in mind. Design structures that can evolve with the family.

**3. TREATING THE FAMILY OFFICE AS INFORMAL OR NON-COMMERCIAL**
**The Issue:** Failing to operationalize the family office with formal processes, documentation, and performance metrics.
**Impact:** Increased risk of errors, inefficiencies, and lack of accountability.
**Solution:** Establish clear policies, procedures, and key performance indicators (KPIs). Treat the office as a professional enterprise.

**4. INADEQUATE RISK MANAGEMENT AND DATA SECURITY**
**The Issue:** Underestimating the importance of cybersecurity, privacy, and comprehensive risk protocols.
**Impact:** Exposure to financial loss, reputational damage, and regulatory breaches.
**Solution:** Implement robust cybersecurity measures, risk assessments, and data protection protocols. Regularly review and update them.

### Licence type definitions (pages 11–12)
> Mauritius offers two primary regulatory structures for family offices under the Financial Services Act 2007: the **Single Family Office (SFO)** and the **Family Office (Multiple) Licence (MFO)**. These structures are designed to support both private family wealth management and commercial multi-family service platforms, with clear regulatory expectations and operational requirements.

> **SINGLE FAMILY OFFICE (SFO)**
> A Single Family Office is a private entity established to manage the wealth and affairs of one family. It is not licensed to provide services to third parties and is typically exempt from full regulatory licensing, provided it does not solicit or manage external funds.
> **Key Features:**
> - Serves only one family group
> - Fully owned and controlled by the family
> - Not open to the public or third-party clients
> - May be exempt from licensing under certain conditions
> - Must maintain internal governance, risk, and compliance protocols

> **FAMILY OFFICE (MULTIPLE) LICENCE (MFO)**
> A licensed MFO is a regulated entity that provides family office services to multiple unrelated families. It is governed under the Financial Services Act 2007 and must meet detailed licensing and operational criteria issued by the FSC.
